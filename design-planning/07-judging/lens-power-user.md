## Lens verdict

**A-instrument best serves the keyboard-first power user** — and by a clear, PROVEN margin: it is the only finalist where the keyboard spine is *actually wired* (palette roving ↑↓, diff and board `j/k`, dual-lens toggle = 3 real handlers + a working component switch), where density is highest yet stays legible (text-sign diff, rationed 4-colour palette, one deduped inbox), and where skeletons resolve in the real layout. D-civic is the runner-up for power users (one real key-nav surface plus the strongest unique switch-test ceiling in the DSR console), while B and C are, as built, keyboard-first in prose only — their ⌘K and `j/k` are `<kbd>` glyphs with zero handlers, making them mouse/chat-first artifacts.

**The most important efficiency weakness across the set:** *the command palette — the supposed power-user spine — is non-functional in 3 of 4 finalists and un-typable in the 4th.* No finalist lets a user press ⌘K, type, fuzzy-filter, and run; A alone wires arrow-key roving inside a pre-opened mock, but even there the input is a fake span. Secondary set-wide gap: **optimistic-update-with-rollback is designed by nobody** (all four explicitly defer it), so the "feels instant on writes" half of D8 is unproven everywhere — every finalist proves read-time perceived-perf (skeletons) but not write-time. A keyboard-first power user would adopt A today and immediately hit the un-typable palette; that is the first thing to fix before any of these ships.
